# 4. The Tag Isn't the Price

> A student in Helsinki works out by hand what a $699.95 used Sony A7 III from a US shop really costs, then shows PriceMonkey getting the same €795 and openly flagging what it can't confirm. It picks an €825 Tori listing, and he says up front that the team makes no money from it and nobody can pay to rank higher.

Avg judge score 6.1 · 252 words · ~110 s

## Teleprompter (spoken only)

This is a used Sony A7 III. This shop says six ninety-nine. That's not the price. I live in Helsinki. That's an American shop, in dollars, so Finland adds VAT on top. Calculator says seven ninety-five. Probably. Shipping? Doesn't say. Do they even ship to Finland? Doesn't say. So now I'm asking a shop for permission to pay them. And that's one shop. Full disclosure. I'm a student. We made this at a hackathon, for Context.dev's challenge, and their tool reads the shop pages. They don't pay us. Right now it makes zero money. It's called PriceMonkey. This is a recording of an earlier search. It finds the shops, then reads each page for the price. This page is an A7R. Not an A7 III. Different camera. Thrown out. This page has two thirty-nine on it. No. Used ones go for about nine fifty. The warning says it's probably another item's price on the page. It shows it, but won't pick it. The six ninety-nine one. Dollars to euros, VAT added. Seven ninety-five. Same as my calculator. Shipping to Finland? Still doesn't say. So, seven ninety-five, plus... nobody knows. It picks a used one on Tori, the Finnish classifieds. Eight twenty-five. More, but it's already in Finland. And it resells, so it's about nine euros a month. The six ninety-nine one, twenty. Private seller, so check it yourself. If we ever earn a fee from a link, it still never gets a shop a better spot. Anyway. Still no word on shipping.

## Shoot notes

CHECK BEFORE ROLLING
1. Time a full read on the phone first. If it runs past 1:52, cut in this order: "The six ninety-nine one, twenty." (pick), then "I'm a student." (disclosure), then "This is a used Sony A7 III." becomes "Used Sony A7 III."
2. "I'm a student": only say it if it's true for the person on camera. Don't change it to "we're students" unless the whole team is.
3. Opening shot: if allenscamera.net loads with the used A7 III at $699.95, film that page. That way the hook is the shop's own tag and not our UI. If it doesn't load, zoom our receipt until only "$699.95 · allenscamera.net" shows.
4. Hunt shot: crop out or hide the credits/cache counter. The replay shows 0 credits and 32 cache hits, and the bible says not to imply it's a live search.
5. mpb card and Allen's receipt: set the browser zoom now so each one fits in one frame (both mpb warnings plus €949 readable; Allen's from $699.95 down to €794.82 plus the shipping flag). If it won't fit, do a slow scroll timed to the VO.
6. Pick shot: the verdict card has to be readable, since the VO now follows it (€825, about €9/mo vs €20/mo). Check where Allen's sits with the all-in toggle on. The VO no longer says "the order is what you'd pay", so either order is fine.
7. Calculator: Google shows 794.98 and the receipt says 794.82. "Probably." covers the gap. Crop the result to about 795 if you can.

WHAT CHANGED AND WHY
- Fee contradiction fixed. The spoken "zero money" now says "right now", and the rule beat says the footer's own words out loud ("earn a fee... never a better spot"). The disclosure no longer promises anything the footer contradicts.
- Removed "make a video about us" (smug), "the order is what you'd pay, all in" (it contradicted the €825 pick), and "See you" (an ending that sounded like an ad).
- The disclosure is down from about 20 s to about 12 s. The fake-out "before I show you anything" is gone, and so is Junction in the VO (the end card has it). Context.dev is said once, plainly: "their tool reads the shop pages". The on-screen text over the hunt ties the replay to Context.dev for the pass/fail rule.
- The camera is named at the top, so "A7R, not an A7 III" makes sense.
- Cut "About twenty sell this camera". It clashed with 17 prices from 24 pages. Only the on-screen 17/24 numbers remain.
- Removed the €239 "Sure." mpb never priced it at €239: the extraction misread the page and the tool caught it. The deadpan "No." is aimed at the number, and the reason is credited to the warning on screen. That way we don't accuse a real shop.
- The pick VO now matches the verdict card (already in Finland, resale, €9 vs €20 a month) and honestly mentions the private-seller trade-off.
- "Reads every page" became "reads each page for the price" (some pages timed out or returned 404).
- Kept "permission to pay them" even though one critic disliked it. Real viewers in the round-3 test loved it and the judge kept it. If it sounds forced when you say it out loud, swap in "So I'd have to email them to find out the real price."
- Kept everything both critiques liked: the hook, "Probably.", the "Doesn't say" twice, "a recording of an earlier search", "Thrown out", "Same as my calculator", "plus... nobody knows", the Tori caption, the callback ending, and the two-setup plan.

## Judge verdicts
- 6: It has the most Rossman instincts. It fact-checks out loud with the €949 median, says "Private seller, so check it yourself," which no one else does, and puts "They don't pay us" up front. That's pro-consumer without preaching. The "Still no word on shipping" callback works. Weaknesses: the problem is one shop ("And that's one shop"), so there's no felt pain of comparing. The disclosure block at 0:27 comes before the viewer knows what "this" is, and it reads like legal copy. The pick section stacks €825, nine a month and twenty in one breath. "I'm a student" is unverified. Context.dev is in a caption and one clause; no endpoint is visibly doing work. It's honest and plain but flat. It needs a second shop in the problem and one fewer number.
- 6: Factually careful and honest. "Used ones go for about nine fifty" matches the €949 median, the caption "17 prices from 24 pages" is accurate, and "Private seller, so check it yourself" is a real Rossman-style pre-emption no other script has. The structure works against it. The problem is one shop and about 20 seconds, then a disclosure block at 0:27 stops the story before the product appears. "They don't pay us" is not sourced in the bible, so it must be true before anyone records it. At 252 words in about 1:46 of speech it is the densest delivery, mostly long sentences, and the pick beat (nine a month, the six ninety-nine one twenty) is stacked numbers again. Context.dev shows up only in a caption over the hunt. The shipping callback at the end is a good sign-off, but overall it reads more like a compliance-safe script than a pitch.
- 6: It has some real Rossman moves. "They don't pay us" and "check it yourself" are the kind of admissions that make me trust someone, and "Used ones go for about nine fifty" is fact-checking out loud. But "That's not the price" as the second line sounds like a lecture before I have any reason to agree. The problem is only one shop ("And that's one shop"), so I never feel the pain of searching twenty. And the full disclosure comes before I've seen the product, so it's an answer to a question I haven't asked yet. The pick section packs resale, per-month cost and a comparison into 16 seconds of voice-over. "Anyway. Still no word on shipping" is a nice dry ending, but Calculator does the same callback with an actual inbox.
- 6.5: It is honest in useful ways. The disclosure comes early and is specific ('They don't pay us. Right now it makes zero money.'). 'Used ones go for about nine fifty' is real fact-checking out loud against the €949 median. 'Private seller, so check it yourself' is the most credible pro-consumer line in any of the drafts. It has only two setups and an on-screen 'pages read with Context.dev' caption, so it is easy to shoot. The problem is that it shows one shop. 'And that's one shop' tells us about the pain but never shows the tab hunt, so the problem lasts barely 27 seconds before the feature tour. 252 words in about 1:46 forces a quick delivery with little room for beats. The per-month comparison ('nine euros a month… The six ninety-nine one, twenty') is said too fast to land. 'I'm a student' is again an unchecked personal claim. It is solid but flat.
