## Final judges
- judge1 7.5: The tightest script here, and the only one that explains the mechanism the bible asks for: free JSON-LD when the page has it, paid Context.dev reads when it doesn't, plus 'I'm not writing a scraper for every shop'. That's honest and it's exactly what the Context.dev judges want to hear. The '€795 + shipping ?' counter is the little-monkey running bit done right. It's a receipt that keeps nagging, not a mascot gag, and 'Still no word on shipping' is a sign-off with no moral. Every Sony number checks against the fixture. It loses points in two places. The PS5 bait is still unsourced per the bible, and it gets dropped after 12 seconds ('That one's easy'), so the cold open is spent on a story the video then walks away from. And 'I built this' makes it a solo project, which is a problem if the team is credited. The problem section is real but thin: we see one shop's missing shipping, not the pain of hunting. Swap or verify the OLX open and it's an 8.
- judge2 7: This one explains the mechanism best. 'Some shops put clean price data right in the page. That's free. The rest, I pay Context.dev to read' is accurate, simple, and the strongest technical execution beat in the set. 'I'm not writing a scraper for every shop' makes Context.dev feel essential. The '€795 + shipping ?' counter is a good running bit, and the Allen's callback ending is plain and dry. The Sony numbers and the '17 offers from 24 pages' caption are correct. The problem is the PS5 OLX bait. The bible marks that story as unverified, and here it's a detour that is waved off ('That one's easy'), so 12 of the first 21 seconds go to something the product never addresses. 'Thirty more than Allen's, but nothing's missing from it' is a bit glib, since tori's shipping and pickup aren't shown either. It has no to-be-fair beat on the product side and no real-time element.
- judge3 6: The running "€795 + shipping ?" counter is a genuinely good device, and the "Still no word on shipping" closer is a dry callback that works. The problem is the open: it baits me with a PS5 scam, then says "I'm not finding out" and drops it after 12 seconds. As an impatient viewer that feels like a bait-and-switch. The Allen's section reads out four numbers in a row (699, 633, 25.5%, 795), and that's where I'd glaze over. "How it reads" is 16 seconds of pipeline explanation I don't care about yet. Tight and honest, but it never gives me a reason to care about camera prices specifically.
- judge4 7: It's tight and easy to shoot: two setups with all audio recorded in one place, which is the smartest note about sound in the whole batch. The receipt is read aloud correctly (633 → +25.5% → 795, shipping unknown). The '€795 + shipping ?' counter that comes back on the Out beat is a real running bit, not a slogan. It's also the only script with the mechanism once and in plain words: free price data in the page, Context.dev for the rest. That's exactly what the bible asks for. What drags it down is the first 12 seconds, which rest on the PS5 OLX story the bible marks as having no source. If the OLX page isn't saved and verified by morning, the cold open is gone and the 'That one's easy' bridge has nothing to bridge from. 'I built this' also leaves the team out. The counter has to be built in the edit, and it appears in three places with an exact cue, which is more post work than it looks. With a verified opener it would score 8.
