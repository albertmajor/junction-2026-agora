## Final judges
- judge1 7: Best problem-first structure. You watch him go through the real pain (Tori, then dollars, the calculator, then Google-translating German classifieds 'for a camera') before PriceMonkey shows up, and the payoff admits its own limit, which is very Rossman. The 'To be fair... I'm not in the US' beat lands, and 'It's their challenge. Not hiding it.' is the right way to name Context.dev. Every fact is sourced (20 shops, South Africa, the A7R set-aside, mpb €239, €794.82). Its weak spot is the one thing The Receipt does well: it never explains how the pages get read. Context.dev is named but never shown pulling a price, and that's close to the pass/fail 'visibly drives something' bar. The 'tab one the whole time' twist could also read to a judge as 'the tool told me what I already had' unless the delivery sells the last line.
- judge2 7: A lean, honest version of the tab race. The sourcing is clean: 20 shops, South Africa, the A7R set aside, mpb €239, €794.82 matching the calculator, and the tori €825 pick. The cold open lands in 5 seconds, and the Kleinanzeigen 'for a camera' beat is a real Rossman-style punch. The ending ('now I know eight twenty-five is a normal price') is a good non-moral payoff. It falls short of tabs-race in two ways. The mechanism is never explained ('reads the actual shop pages' is all we get, with no free-vs-paid split and no visible extraction), so the Best-Use-of-Context.dev criterion rests on a disclosure line. And 'one box … puts every price on one line. In euros, tax in.' edges toward feature-tour copy. The money section and sign-off are solid.
- judge3 7: This is the only one where I recognised myself in the first 15 seconds. Five tabs, a calculator, then Google-translating German classifieds "for a camera" is exactly my Saturday. It moves fast and every cut earns its spot. The A7R aside is short enough that I didn't skip. The payoff is a joke at the narrator's own expense instead of a sales line, and that's why I stayed for the money bit. Points off: the cold open "Not for me, it isn't" is too cryptic to hook me before I know he's in Finland, and "twenty shops, as far as South Africa" plus "in euros, tax in" is a feature list squeezed into one breath. Still the one I'd watch to the end.
- judge4 8: This is the one I'd most want to shoot tomorrow. It needs three simple recordings. The Chrome hunt is real footage that I can stage in ten minutes, and nothing in it depends on a fixture we haven't seen. Every number matches the bible: $699.95, +25.5% VAT, €795, mpb €239 at 75% under, the A7R set aside, 20 shops including South Africa, and tori €825 as a 'typical' price. The structure has a payoff, because the same tab is both the problem and the answer. 'Same as my calculator' makes the product check the presenter's own work instead of just claiming things. Two weaknesses. Context.dev is only named in the voice-over: the mechanism (free JSON-LD first, a paid read after) is never shown, and that hurts the 'visibly drives the demo' pass/fail rule. The 5-second cold open is also thin, because 'Not for me, it isn't' needs the viewer to already know he's in Finland. The money beat is fine but generic. I'd add a 2-second insert of a receipt line naming its source and keep everything else.
