## Final judges
- judge1 5: It has the most original angle, one English search that reads Swedish, German and Dutch ads, and it's the only one that shows the watch, which helps with real-timeness. The chatbot line is honest and funny. But it's built on sand. The PS5 open is unsourced, and the Monark 1 200 SEK card, the €9 bike and the €50–100 shipping estimate aren't in the bible's verified facts. The bike hunt also carries the known false-positive flags and '32 listings abroad are pickup only', so a costed Swedish bike with estimated shipping may simply not be what the app shows. 'The cheap one's always in another country' is a slogan-ish generalisation. The three-beat feature walk (watch, pick, fee) drifts into a tour. If the bike numbers can't be filmed exactly as written, it falls apart.
- judge2 5.5: It has the most interesting framing: cross-border and cross-language, an English query fanning out into kaupunkipyörä and stadscykel. It's the only script with a watch, which touches the real-timeness criterion, and it shows Context.dev pointing at where it read '1200 SEK', the best visible-extraction moment of all six. But much of it isn't in the bible. The multi-language search, the Monark SEK card, the €9 bike, and the 'est. €50–100' shipping row are all unsourced. That last one conflicts with '32 listings abroad are pickup only'. The bike search's flags are the known false-positive area, so leaning on the €9 'too good to be true' flag is risky. It reuses the unverified PS5 story as its hook, and 'I had to ask a chatbot' invites a sideways question. Every claim needs to be checked against the fixture before shooting.
- judge3 5.5: The thesis line is the clearest problem statement of any script. The multilingual search (kaupunkipyörä, stadscykel...) is the most "oh, that's new" visual here. But the PS5 open never pays off, and the product demo is a €60 city bike, which is low-stakes. I don't feel ripped off over a Jopo. The Swedish bike shipping-guess section is honest but slow ("Is the shipping real? No. It's a guess"). The watch feature at 1:20 feels tacked on. "Didn't buy it. That's it." is a fine deadpan exit. Also, the €9 bike and the Monark 1200 SEK aren't in the fact sheet, so check them.
- judge4 5: It has the strongest framing of the problem and the impact (one English query, ads read in four languages), and the €60 tori pick matches the bible. But as a director I can't promise the shots. The Monark 1200 SEK card, the 'est. €50–100' shipping row, the €183 total and the €9 flagged bike appear nowhere in the bible. The bible also warns that the flags on the bike search are partly false positives, so presenting a flagged €9 bike is exactly the risky claim we were told to avoid. The PS5 open adds a second unverified story ('asked a chatbot'), and the bike pivot leaves that cold open with nothing to pay off. If any of those cards doesn't exist on the replay tomorrow, half the script falls apart. Verify every bike card before this gets near a camera.
