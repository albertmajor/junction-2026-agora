## Spoken only
This camera says six ninety-nine. That's not what it costs.

I'm in Finland. That's dollars, and about a quarter on top in import VAT. Shipping? Nobody says. Do they even ship to Finland? Doesn't say. It's a shop. Sending me things is the whole job.

So I built PriceMonkey. It uses an API called Context dot dev to find the shops and actually read each listing page: the price, whether it's in stock.

A lot of what it reads is garbage. Wrong model, dead page, it gets tossed. And this one. Two thirty-nine euros, for a camera that goes for about nine fifty used. That's probably some other item's price off the same page. A dumb scraper tells you that's the cheapest one. This shows it with a warning, and it doesn't count.

Then it flips everything to what you'd pay here. That six ninety-nine is six thirty-three in euros. Add import VAT: seven ninety-five. Shipping? It can't get that out of them either. So it just says it doesn't know.

What it tells me to buy is a used one on tori. Eight twenty-five. More than the import, but it's already here. Sell it in a year for about seven fifteen, and it's cost me nine euros a month. The import one, about twenty a month.

How I'd make money: affiliate links. Not live yet. If you buy through it, the shop might pay me a cut. That never moves anyone up the list. The order is what you pay, all in, and every number has a receipt you can open. If that ever changes, call me out.

Still no shipping price. See you.

## Final judges
- judge1 6: It has the most real voice of the six. The run-on sentences, then the punches, and the single line of real heat ('Sending me things is the whole job') are how Rossman actually sounds, not a costume. Facts are clean, and the way it handles the mpb €239 row ('A dumb scraper tells you that's the cheapest one') is good. But it has no story. There's no hunt, no tabs and no 'I want this camera', so the problem is told rather than lived. It also does the Allen's math twice (0:00 and 0:52), which wastes 15 seconds. The money section runs 20 seconds with a sermon-ish 'every number has a receipt you can open', and 'Credits counter out of frame' feels like hiding the cached run. Solid, but forgettable next to the tab scripts.
- judge2 6.5: It has a strong voice and disciplined sourcing: the opener 'That's not what it costs', one line of real heat, and the 'dumb scraper' contrast on mpb. The numbers are correct, and 'every number has a receipt you can open' is a good pro-consumer line. But the problem is a single Allen's page with no story or stakes behind it, and there's no race or twist, so the middle reads like a guided tour. It covers the set-aside list, mpb, the receipt, a verdict with per-month math, and money, much of it delivered as numbers. Context.dev gets one line ('find the shops and actually read each listing page'), with no free-vs-paid split. The note to keep the credits counter out of frame hides the 0-credit replay instead of disclosing it. The other Sony scripts are fairly open about the cached recording, so this looks evasive if a judge notices. 'See you' is a fine sign-off.
- judge3 5: It has one great line of real heat, and the rest is flat to the point of sleepy. Opening on a zoomed receipt line is the weakest first frame of the six. There's no person and no story, just a number. The middle is a long VO walkthrough of garbage pages, mpb and the conversion math, with little visual change. Then a 20-second money monologue ("every number has a receipt you can open") turns into a lecture right when I'm ready to leave. No tabs, no PS5, no moment where I see myself in it. Competent, honest, forgettable.
- judge4 6.5: It has the best single line and the most real voice of any script here. The run-ons followed by short punches really sound like Rossman, and every number is accurate (€949 median, 715 resale, €9 vs €20 a month). The shoot list is minimal. As a pitch it's weaker. Junction and the team never come up. The end card says 'built on Context.dev' with no mention of the hackathon. The money section is a 20-second talking head that will sag, and the opening is a static zoom on a receipt with no visual change for 5 seconds. 'Credits counter out of frame' is technically honest but reads like hiding the cached run. A visible 'replayed' caption would be better. The mechanism is described ('find the shops and actually read each listing page') but not shown. The tone is great, but the structure feels like a monologue with B-roll.
