# Lessons from round 3's fresh-viewer test (Rossman fans who know nothing about the product)
1. Naming Context.dev reads like a SPONSOR READ and makes viewers skip. But it's the challenge, it must be credited. FIX: be Rossman-honest about it, ONCE, plainly: e.g. "Full disclosure: we built this at Junction, a hackathon in Helsinki, for Context.dev's challenge. Their thing is what actually reads the shop pages." Then show it working. Never "powered by" language, never repeat the name 4x (end card is fine).
2. "We" vs "I" confusion and "why is a guy in Finland on Polish OLX?" -> say who we are early and simply (students at a hackathon in Helsinki). Pick one voice.
3. Jargon kills it: "free reads", "JSON-LD", "flagged", "not ranked", "used median", "replayed", "lane", "landed". Say it how a normal person would ("it ignores that one: way too cheap, probably the price of something else on the page").
4. Too many numbers/currencies in a row confuses. Max ONE number per sentence; prefer euros; let the screen carry the rest.
5. PS5 hook problem: the saved OLX page's 'Zobacz też' strip ALSO shows Pros at 2 250 zł and 1 350 zł, so "3,100 vs 1,600" falls apart if you scroll. Don't scroll; use the 3 100 zł card as a still, or drop the comparison and just use the 1,600 zł + zero ratings bait.
6. Lines viewers loved (keep the spirit): "Sure." deadpan on the €239 too-cheap one; "plus... nobody knows" on shipping; "So now I'm asking a shop for permission to pay them."; "Is that good? No idea."; Google-translating German classifieds; "This one says A7 III. It's an A7R. Different camera. Thrown out."; doing the import math on a calculator then the app matching it ("Same as my calculator").
7. Lines they refused to say: "I'm not saying X, I'm saying Y", "It's not X, it's Y", "genuinely sat there going", "literally", "one search, one tab" slogans, self-deprecating name jokes, "congratulations" sarcasm unless flat.
