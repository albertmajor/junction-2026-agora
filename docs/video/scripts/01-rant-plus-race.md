# 1. Tab One (locked v5)

> A guy in Finland spends four tabs, a calculator and Google Translate pricing a used Sony A7 III. PriceMonkey, built at Junction for Context.dev's challenge, reads 20 shops, throws out the wrong camera, refuses to pick the too-cheap fake price, and lands on the listing he opened first.

Avg judge score 7.3 · 269 words · ~107 s

## Teleprompter (spoken only)

I want a used Sony A7 three. I live in Finland. Finding out what one costs took me a calculator and Google Translate.
Tab one. Tori, Finnish classifieds. Eight hundred twenty-five euros. Is that good? No idea.
This American shop. Six ninety-nine. Dollars.
Convert it, add Finnish VAT. Seven ninety-five euros.
Shipping to Finland? Doesn't say. So I'd have to email them and ask if they'll take my money.
And now I'm Google-translating German ads. For a camera. Okay.
Quick note. We built this at Junction, a hackathon in Helsinki, for Context.dev's challenge. Their API is the part that reads the shop pages.
This is PriceMonkey. Recorded earlier, not live. It checked twenty shops and lined up every price, in euros, tax included.
Click any price and it shows you the exact page it came from.
This one came up for A7 three. It's an A7R. Different camera. Thrown out.
MPB. Two thirty-nine. Sure. Way too cheap, probably the price of something else on that page. It still shows it. It just won't pick it.
Allen's. Seven ninety-five. Same as my calculator. Plus shipping, which is... nobody knows. And it says so.
To be fair to Allen's, six ninety-nine is a real price. In the US. I'm not in the US.
It picked Tori. Thirty euros more, but it's here, and I can go look at it first.
It was tab one the whole time. But now I know eight twenty-five is normal, and I didn't need the calculator.
We make no money from this. If we ever take a referral fee, it won't buy anyone a better spot.

## Judge verdicts
- 7: It's the cleanest and most human of the five. There's one real purchase, 33 seconds of the problem before any product, the math done by hand on screen, a fair "to be fair", and the numbers stay light. "It was tab one the whole time. But now I know eight twenty-five is normal" is honest about what the tool actually gave him: confidence, not a cheaper price. That's the pro-consumer point done right, and no other cut lands it as well. It's the least sloppy. Weaknesses: Context.dev is underplayed for a challenge entry. It gets a caption and "the part that reads the shop pages", and nothing on screen proves their data drove the result, so show a source or the via-contextdev line on the click-a-price beat. The disclosure comes before the product has a name, so "we built this" has nothing to point to. The German-ads beat is filler. There's no monkey beyond the end card. Of the five it's the safest to ship.
- 7: This has the best problem section of the five. Google-translating German ads "for a camera" is the moment I'd recognise from my own life, and the open is short, personal and specific. The ending, "It was tab one the whole time", is honest and doesn't oversell, and I respect that. But the "Quick note" disclosure at 0:33 stops the momentum before I even know what the product is, so it reads like a sponsor read dropped in the middle. Admitting the pick was the first tab also nearly argues against the product. "Now I know eight twenty-five is normal" saves it, but only just. The MPB section is good. "Click any price and it shows you the exact page" is a feature-list line. It's solid and believable but a little flat after the strong open.
- 8: This is the one I'd shoot this morning. It has three recordings and one phone spot, and every number is in the fixture: the calculator gives 794.81 against the app's €794.82, the €825 sits in the typical band and 20 shops is correct. The arc pays off. It opens on 'Is that good? No idea' and closes on 'now I know eight twenty-five is normal'. That gives the problem 33 seconds told in the first person, which fixes v1's biggest failure. The pick is justified in plain words ('it's here, and I can go look at it first') rather than a €9.16/mo pile, so it avoids the v1 number crowding. It still has weak spots. The 'Quick note' disclosure at 0:33 is a dead patch in the middle. Context.dev shows up only as a caption and a spoken credit, never as something visibly reading a page, which leaves the Best Use criterion thin. A 'source' line on the click-through would fix that. 'Recorded earlier, not live' sounds defensive. The pro-consumer and scam angle is mild, and the monkey gets only the end card. It is honest, tight and filmable before 10:00.
